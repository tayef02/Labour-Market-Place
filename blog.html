For workers

Agree the wage before you start, not after

3 minutes to read

The commonest complaint on this site is a wage that shrank by the end of the day. Almost every one of them starts the same way: the work began before anyone said a number out loud.

Before you pick up a tool, say the figure back to the person hiring you and get a yes. Ask how many days the work will run and what happens if it rains. If food or tools were promised, say that out loud too. The job post on this site already carries the wage the employer offered, so open it on your phone and point at it.

For employers

A good job post gets you better hands

2 minutes to read

Posts that say only "need a mason" sit unanswered. Posts that say the trade, the daily wage, how many people, how many days, and exactly where the site is get answered within hours.

Be honest about the hard parts of the work. A worker who turns up and finds the job heavier than he was told will leave, and you will have lost a day. Write the landmark nearest the site, not just the union name, so people can find you without ringing you five times.

How this works

What your rating means

2 minutes to read

When an employer closes a job, he can give every worker he hired a rating out of five and write a line about them. That rating shows on your card on the Find Workers page, so an employer looking for a mason in your district sees it before he calls you.

Nobody can rate you unless they actually hired you on a job that has been closed, and each employer can rate you once per job. Turn up on time, finish what you started, and the rating takes care of itself.
