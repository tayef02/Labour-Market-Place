About this project

Why it exists, who it is for, and how it was built.

The problem

In most towns in Bangladesh a day labourer stands at a labour point every morning and waits for someone to come and hire him. He does not know what work is available that day, what the going rate is, or whether the person hiring him will pay what was agreed. An employer who needs four helpers at short notice has the same trouble in reverse: he must go to the labour point and hope the right trade is standing there.

Between the two there is usually a middle man, and he takes a cut of a wage that is already small.

What this site does

An employer posts the work and the daily wage he is offering. A worker sees the posts that match his trade and his district, applies to the ones that suit him, and the two talk by phone and agree between themselves. The site does not touch the money and takes nothing from the wage.

After the work is finished the employer rates the worker, so a worker who turns up on time and does clean work builds something he can show.

Who uses it

  • Worker Finds work, applies, builds a rating
  • Employer Posts jobs, picks workers, rates them
  • Super Admin Everything, including making other admins
  • Moderator Admin Approves or rejects job posts, blocks users
  • Support Admin Answers complaints, can see users but not block

How it was built

  • Front end Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  • Database Supabase (PostgreSQL)
  • Tables users, admins, jobs, applications, complaints, ratings
  • Session Browser localStorage
  • Framework None, and no build step

Written for a Computer Science and Engineering course project at Sylhet International University.

What is still to come

Passwords are stored as plain text and need to be hashed. The database access rules are open and need to be tightened per role. Phone numbers are not verified by OTP. Beyond security, the plan is SMS and WhatsApp alerts when a job is approved, a map view of where the work is, photographs on worker profiles, and a Bengali version of every page.
